## Why Data Science in Social Contexts Matters
  Before taking this course, I mostly thought about data science as working with numbers, coding, and finding patterns in datasets. But I am starting to understand that data science becomes much more complicated when the data represents real people and real social issues. A model might look like just numbers on a computer, but those numbers can represent someone’s income, education, health, opportunities, or experiences. Because of that, I think learning data science in a social and interdisciplinary context is really important.
  One reason we model social topics and people is because it can help us understand patterns that would be difficult to notice otherwise. For example, data could help show differences in access to education, transportation, healthcare, or jobs between different communities. Models can also help organizations make predictions and decisions based on large amounts of information. At the same time, I do not think a model can perfectly represent a person or their situation. People are much more complicated than a few variables in a dataset, so it is important to understand the limits of what a model is actually telling us.
  I also think there are a lot of responsibilities that come with using data about people. Privacy is one of the first things that comes to mind. People should know how their information is being collected and used, especially when the data is personal. Another responsibility is making sure that the data or model is not unfairly biased toward certain groups. If the original data already contains inequalities or missing information, a model could accidentally repeat or even strengthen those problems. Data scientists should also be careful about how they explain their results instead of presenting a prediction as if it is automatically the truth.
  Through this course, I am hoping to understand more about how models actually affect decisions in the real world. I want to learn how to recognize bias, question the data being used, and understand when a model is useful versus when it might be misleading. Since I am studying data science, I think learning how to build models is important, but learning when and how they should be used responsibly is just as important. I hope this course helps me look beyond the technical side of data science and think more carefully about the people behind the data.
